1. Executive Summary

During manual testing, I observed inconsistent follow-state behavior on an Instagram profile. After tapping "Follow back," the button reportedly changes briefly to "Following" and then reverts to "Follow back." The displayed follower count appeared to increase after repeated taps.

The increased count persisted after refreshing the profile, and the follower list did not show a corresponding new unique follower for each increase. Screenshots captured during testing show the same displayed profile and handle (rakhi245694) with follower counts of 0, 2, and 10.

This is a potential follower-count integrity and UI consistency issue. The screenshots show differing displayed counts but do not independently prove that repeated taps caused every change. The underlying technical cause remains unconfirmed.

2. Problem Statement

The observed behavior raises a question about whether the displayed follower count remains consistent with the set of unique followers and whether the follow button accurately reflects the current relationship state.

4. Expected Behavior
  • A follower/followee pair should represent no more than one follow relationship.
  • Repeated follow requests should not inflate the displayed follower count.
  • The displayed count should accurately reflect the unique follower set, subject to documented platform caching or update delays.
  • The button should reflect the server-confirmed relationship state, such as "Following" or "Requested," as applicable.
3. Reproduction Procedure (Controlled Accounts)
  1. Use two Instagram accounts controlled by the researcher (Account A and Account B).
  2. Have Account A follow Account B.
  3. Log in to Account B and open Account A’s profile.
  4. Note the displayed follower count and confirm that "Follow back" is visible.
  5. Tap "Follow back" once and observe the button state.
  6. Wait approximately one second and check whether the button reverts to "Follow back."
  7. If the behavior occurs, repeat only a small number of times and record the displayed count.
  8. Refresh the profile and check whether the count persists.
  9. Inspect the follower list to determine whether each count change corresponds to a new unique follower.
5. Observed / Reported Behavior
  • The button reportedly transitions briefly to "Following," then returns to "Follow back."
  • The displayed follower count reportedly increases after repeated taps and persists after refreshing the profile.
  • The researcher reports that the follower list does not show a corresponding new unique follower for each increase.
  • Three screenshots of the same displayed profile show counts of 2, 0, and 10. These are separate captures, not a continuous recording of a causal sequence.
  • In the screenshot showing 10 followers, the numeral appears unusually small relative to nearby text. This may be a separate rendering/layout anomaly; its cause and relationship to the count issue are unknown.
6. Potential Impact

If confirmed, the issue could reduce the integrity and reliability of follower counts displayed on profiles. This could affect users or third parties who rely on follower counts as a public popularity metric.

No impact on recommendations, ranking, monetization, notifications, or other platform systems has been verified. Repeated notifications have not been confirmed.

7. Technical Assessment & Limitations

The evidence supports investigating a discrepancy between displayed follower counts, follow-button state, and the unique follower list.

It does not establish that duplicate server-side relationship records exist, nor does it identify whether the cause is server-side counter processing, client-side optimistic state, caching, delayed synchronization, or another mechanism. A continuous video recording showing the initial state, each tap, the refreshed state, and the follower list would strengthen causal evidence.

8. Suggested Remediation
  • Investigate the source of truth used to calculate and display follower counts.
  • Make follow operations idempotent so retries or repeated requests cannot create multiple relationships or increment counters more than once.
  • Enforce uniqueness for each (follower_id, followee_id) pair.
  • Reconcile displayed counts against the authoritative set of unique follower relationships.
  • Ensure the UI reflects server-confirmed follow state and investigate the smaller numeral layout separately.
9. Evidence Inventory
  • Evidence 1: Screenshot showing 2 followers & "Follow back" button.
  • Evidence 2: Screenshot showing 0 followers & "Follow back" button.
  • Evidence 3: Screenshot showing 10 followers, "Follow back" button, & visibly smaller numeral.

Defensive Engineering Perspective: How Distributed Social Graphs Prevent Counter Drift

While the root cause inside Instagram's production infrastructure remains unconfirmed (and could involve client-side optimistic UI state, local cache invalidation, or async edge counter lag), designing a resilient social-graph follow endpoint requires strict Idempotency Keys, Composite Primary-Key Uniqueness, and Authoritative Set Reconciliation.

01 // Composite Graph Uniqueness

Enforce a strict unique constraint on (follower_id, followee_id) in the backing graph store so retries or rapid taps execute an atomic INSERT ... ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING rather than appending duplicate edges.

02 // Idempotent Counter Mutation

Increment materialized follower counters only when the underlying edge state transition 0 → 1 commits, rather than on raw HTTP request receipt or optimistic client tap events.

03 // Client State Reconciliation

Bind the mobile client’s "Follow back" / "Following" button state and displayed follower count strictly to the server-signed response payload, rolling back optimistic increments if the mutation rolls back.
